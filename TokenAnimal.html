Same Token, Different Animal

Does turning a word into a token depend on the other words in the prompt? Is “animal” the same token in “The big animal roamed the prairie” and “The big man of an animal came through the door”?

No. The token is identical in both sentences. What changes is what the model makes of it, and that happens later.

Step one · The tokenizer

A dictionary lookup that ignores the sentence

The tokenizer chops text into pieces and looks each piece up in a fixed vocabulary. It never reads ahead or behind. In both sentences, “animal” comes after a space, so it becomes the same piece, " animal", with the same ID number.

Sentence A
The#··· big#··· animal#4512 roamed#··· the#··· prairie#···
Sentence B
The#··· big#··· man#··· of#··· an#··· animal#4512 came#··· through#··· the#··· door#···

The ID 4512 is illustrative. Each model has its own vocabulary and numbering, but within one model the two “animal” tokens always share a number.

What can change the token

Only the word’s own surface

The neighbors never matter. The only things that change the token are the surface details of the word itself.

Token numbers are illustrative. “␣” marks the space that belongs to the token.

Leading space

" animal" mid-sentence and "animal" at the start of a line are usually different tokens.

Capitals

"Animal" and "animal" can be different tokens.

Attached marks

In "animal," or "animal’s" the punctuation can change how the text is split.

Steps two and three · Where meaning enters

One starting point, two destinations

  1. Token. “animal” becomes ID 4512 in both sentences.
  2. Embedding. The ID is swapped for a fixed list of numbers, its starting vector. Still identical in both sentences.
  3. Attention layers. Layer after layer, each word takes in information from the words around it. Now the two “animals” pull apart: one toward a literal creature on the prairie, the other toward a large, rough man at a door.

Schematic only. Real models use thousands of dimensions; the picture shows the idea that the two paths share a start and separate as layers add context.

An analogy from physics

The bare particle and the dressed particle

The token is like a bare particle with fixed intrinsic properties. Attention “dresses” it with interactions from its surroundings, the way an electron moving through a crystal behaves differently from a free electron, even though it is the same particle.

The spelling and the token are fixed. The understanding is built from context.
