Two ideas explain how a machine that understands nothing can appear to understand a great deal.

Break the object of study into atomistic descriptions. Words, numbers, and images all become patterns of 0s and 1s. Then implement processes that can understand and operate at that level of description.

Speed and scale: overcoming brute force

Brute force means checking every item, one after another. The machine does not avoid that work. It makes the work cheap with speed of computation and scale of data storage.

Putting the two ideas together

  1. Break the object of study into atoms: 0s and 1s.
  2. Build simple processes that operate on those atoms.
  3. Run them at great speed, over a great deal of stored data.

Simple steps, repeated enormously fast across enormous stores, can look like understanding.

Addendum

Meaning: the missing ingredient

Bits, speed, and scale are not enough.

Everything above describes a machine that can handle symbols quickly and in vast quantity. But a machine that only shuffles 0s and 1s has no way to know that a dog is more like a wolf than like a carburetor, or that bank means one thing beside river and another beside loan. Speed and storage supply the capacity. They do not supply the meaning.

What large language models added is a record of semantic relations: how words, ideas, and phrases connect to one another, learned from an enormous body of human writing. Each word is placed among its neighbors by how it is actually used. That web of relations is the layer that lets a machine move from handling symbols to working with what they mean.

That is why the Rosetta Stone fits. It did not give scholars more hieroglyphs or faster scribes. It gave them a bridge, because the same meaning was written in a script they could already read. In the same way, the semantic relations captured by LLMs form a bridge between the ordinary language people use and the binary machinery underneath.

Bits give us atoms. Speed and scale give us power. Semantic relations give us a way to connect that power to meaning, and that connection looks essential to any real digital intelligence.
